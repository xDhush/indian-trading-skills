HDFC Bank, founded in 1994 and headquartered in Mumbai, is India's #1 private sector bank by assets (Rs. 48.19 lakh Cr), deposits, and advances. It serves 93+ million customers through 8,700+ branches. In July 2023, it completed a landmark merger with parent HDFC Ltd, making it one of the world's largest banks by market cap.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

1. **Cheapest it's been in years.** At 16.9x trailing PE (forward PE just 13.2x), the stock trades at a 30% discount to its 5-year average PE of 22–24x. For India's highest-quality private bank, this is rare.
2. **Merger synergies are coming through.** The combined entity cross-sells home loans to 93M banking customers. Operating efficiency is improving as systems integrate.
3. **Rock-solid asset quality.** Bad loans (GNPA) at 1.24% — among the lowest in Indian banking — and falling. The bank barely has any stressed assets.
4. **Fortress balance sheet.** Capital adequacy ratio of 19.9% is nearly double the regulatory minimum of 11.9%. No need for dilutive equity raises.
5. **Wall Street loves it.** 35 out of 38 analysts say Buy, with targets ranging from Rs. 858 to Rs. 1,575.

### Bear Case

1. **Margins have compressed.** Net interest margin (NIM) — the spread between what the bank earns on loans vs. pays on deposits — dropped to 3.35% from 4%+ pre-merger, because HDFC Ltd's borrowings are costlier than retail deposits.
2. **Cheap deposits ratio has fallen.** CASA ratio (current + savings accounts, which are low-cost funding) is at 33.6%, down from 40%+ before the merger. Rebuilding this will take years.
3. **Foreign investors are selling.** FII holding dropped from 48.4% to 47.7% in just one quarter. This selling is adding downward pressure on the stock.
4. **Loan growth may slow.** RBI is cautious on unsecured lending, and the broader economy faces headwinds from global trade uncertainty.

### Base Case

HDFC Bank delivers 12–15% annual profit growth over the next 2–3 years as merger benefits kick in, margins recover to 3.5–3.7%, and the cheap deposits base rebuilds. At current prices, the stock re-rates toward 18–20x earnings over 12–18 months.

## Conclusion

HDFC Bank is India's highest-quality banking franchise, going through a temporary rough patch due to the HDFC Ltd merger integration. The stock has sold off 24% this year, pushing valuations to multi-year lows. But the fundamentals tell a different story: profits are growing at 11.5%, bad loans are at just 1.24% and falling, the bank has nearly twice the capital it needs, and cash flow generation is excellent.

**The opportunity in simple terms:** You're getting India's best bank at its cheapest price in years. The merger headwinds (lower margins, lower cheap deposits) are temporary and well-understood. As these normalize over the next 1–2 years, the stock should re-rate back toward its historical valuation.

**What to watch:**
- NIM recovering toward 3.5%+ (currently 3.35%)
- CASA ratio moving back above 35% (currently 33.6%)
- GNPA staying below 1.5% (currently 1.24%)
- FII selling stabilizing (currently -0.7pp per quarter)

**Review triggers:** Re-evaluate if GNPA rises above 1.5%, NIM falls below 3.0%, or the stock breaks Rs. 700 on heavy volume.

**Bottom line:** For long-term investors (1–3 year horizon), this is a high-conviction buy. For traders, wait for a technical reversal signal (RSI bounce above 40, or price reclaiming Rs. 830) before entering.
