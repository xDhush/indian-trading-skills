# Financial Terms Glossary

Plain-English explanations for common financial terms. Used by the Report Writer to add "What It Means" context in reports.

Only apply these when the term appears in a table or as a key metric. Do not explain terms that are self-evident (Revenue, Profit, Market Cap, Share Price, Volume, Dividend).

---

## Valuation Ratios

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| PE Ratio (Price to Earnings) | How many years of current earnings the stock price reflects. Lower = cheaper. |
| Forward PE | Same as PE, but based on expected future earnings. |
| PB Ratio (Price to Book) | Price relative to the company's net asset value per share. |
| EV/EBITDA | Company's total value (including debt) relative to operating profit. Capital-structure neutral. |
| PEG Ratio | PE adjusted for growth — below 1.0 means growth justifies the price. |
| Price to Sales | Market cap relative to revenue. Useful for loss-making companies. |
| Earnings Yield | Inverse of PE — the return you'd get if all earnings were paid out. Compare with FD rates. |
| Dividend Yield | Annual dividend as a percentage of the stock price. |

## Profitability Ratios

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| ROE (Return on Equity) | How much profit the company generates per rupee of shareholder money. |
| ROA (Return on Assets) | How efficiently the company uses all its assets to generate profit. |
| ROCE (Return on Capital Employed) | Returns generated on all long-term capital (equity + debt). |
| ROIC (Return on Invested Capital) | True return on all invested capital. Above 12% = creating value. |
| Operating Margin | Percentage of revenue left after operating costs, before interest and tax. |
| Net Margin | Bottom-line profit as a percentage of revenue. |
| EBITDA Margin | Operating profitability before depreciation and interest — useful for comparing across companies. |

## Leverage & Solvency

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| Debt to Equity | How much debt the company has relative to shareholders' money. Lower = safer. |
| Interest Coverage | How many times the company can pay its interest bill from operating profit. Higher = safer. |
| Current Ratio | Can the company pay its short-term bills? Above 1.5 = comfortable. |
| Quick Ratio | Same as current ratio but excludes inventory — a stricter test. |
| Net Debt | Total debt minus cash on hand. Negative = more cash than debt. |

## Cash Flow

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| Operating Cash Flow (OCF) | Cash generated from the core business (not paper profit, actual cash). |
| Free Cash Flow (FCF) | Cash left after maintaining/expanding the business. The real "spendable" profit. |
| OCF/PAT Ratio | Checks if reported profits are backed by real cash. Above 1.0 = strong. |
| Capex | Money spent on buying/upgrading physical assets (factories, equipment, offices). |
| FCF Yield | Free cash flow as a percentage of market cap. Like earnings yield but cash-based. |

## Banking-Specific

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| NIM (Net Interest Margin) | The spread between what a bank earns on loans and pays on deposits. Core profitability measure. |
| Gross NPA | Percentage of total loans that have gone bad (borrower stopped paying). |
| Net NPA | Bad loans after deducting provisions (money set aside for expected losses). |
| CASA Ratio | Proportion of deposits in current and savings accounts — these are cheap for the bank. Higher = better margins. |
| CAR (Capital Adequacy Ratio) | Buffer the bank holds against potential losses. Regulatory minimum is 11.9%. |
| CET1 | Highest-quality capital (common equity). The core safety buffer. |
| Provision Coverage Ratio | How much of bad loans is covered by provisions. Above 70% = comfortable. |
| Credit Cost | Annual provisioning as a percentage of loans. Lower = better asset quality. |
| CASA Deposit | Current Account + Savings Account deposits — low-cost funding source for banks. |
| Slippage Ratio | Fresh bad loans as a percentage of total good loans. Measures asset quality deterioration. |

## Technical Analysis

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| RSI (Relative Strength Index) | Momentum score from 0–100. Below 30 = oversold (may bounce). Above 70 = overbought (may pull back). |
| MACD | Trend-following indicator. Bullish when MACD line crosses above signal line. |
| SMA (Simple Moving Average) | Average price over N days. 200 DMA = long-term trend line. |
| EMA (Exponential Moving Average) | Like SMA but gives more weight to recent prices. |
| Bollinger Bands | Price channel based on volatility. Price near lower band = oversold, upper band = overbought. |
| ATR (Average True Range) | Measures daily price volatility in rupees. Higher ATR = bigger daily swings. |
| Death Cross | 50-day average crosses below 200-day average — bearish long-term signal. |
| Golden Cross | 50-day average crosses above 200-day average — bullish long-term signal. |
| Support | Price level where buyers tend to step in. A floor. |
| Resistance | Price level where sellers tend to step in. A ceiling. |
| OBV (On-Balance Volume) | Cumulative volume indicator. Rising OBV with rising price confirms the trend. |
| ADX | Trend strength score. Above 25 = strong trend. Below 20 = no clear trend. |
| SuperTrend | Trend indicator that flips between bullish and bearish. Commonly used in Indian markets. |

## Shareholding (India-Specific)

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| Promoter Holding | Percentage of shares held by the company's founders/controlling family. |
| Promoter Pledge | Shares pledged by promoters as loan collateral. High pledge = risk of forced selling if price drops. |
| FII (Foreign Institutional Investor) | Foreign funds, banks, and institutions investing in India. |
| DII (Domestic Institutional Investor) | Indian mutual funds, insurance companies (LIC), pension funds. |
| Free Float | Shares available for public trading (total minus promoter + locked-in shares). |
| CASA | Current Account Savings Account — refers to low-cost bank deposits. |

## Other

| Term | Plain English |
|------|--------------|
| Beta | How much the stock moves relative to the market. Below 1 = less volatile than Nifty. |
| Face Value | Nominal value of a share (Rs. 1, 2, 5, or 10). Used for dividends and splits. |
| Market Cap | Total value of all shares = share price x number of shares. |
| TTM | Trailing twelve months — the last 4 quarters combined. |
| YTD | Year to date — performance from January 1 to today. |
| CAGR | Compound Annual Growth Rate — smoothed annual return over multiple years. |
| Cr (Crore) | Indian numbering: 1 Crore = 10 million. |
| L (Lakh) | Indian numbering: 1 Lakh = 100,000. |
| bps (basis points) | 1 bps = 0.01%. So 18 bps = 0.18%. |
| FY | Fiscal Year in India: April to March. FY25 = April 2024 – March 2025. |
